Droplet Generation Using Centrifugal Microfluidics

Final-year project (CUHK) · Prof. Aaron Ho team
Built & tested

What

  • A two-generation centrifugal microfluidic platform (Thesis I → II, CUHK BME final year project) integrated with a silicon micropore for generating microdroplets, targeting diagnostics, digital PCR, and material synthesis applications.
  • ADDMS, a companion MATLAB image-analysis pipeline, to enhance microscope imagery and automatically detect, count, and size droplets, replacing manual measurement.

How

  • PDMS chips CAD-modeled in SolidWorks, CNC-machined, and bonded via oxygen plasma; the first single-chamber design (GEN6) stopped producing droplets above 250 rpm, so I redesigned to a three-compartment chip (GEN10) that kept each fluid stable feeding the micropore.
  • GEN10 generated droplets across the full 200–800 rpm range and 30/50/60 µm pore sizes, where GEN6 failed beyond 250 rpm — up to 1,570 droplets counted in a single 50 µm-pore sample at 800 rpm.
  • ADDMS enhances each raw microscope frame (AI upscaling, non-local means denoising, bicubic upscaling, sharpening, adaptive histogram equalization, Wiener filtering), then counts the full frame via Canny edge detection with watershed segmentation to separate touching droplets (1,570 detected and bounded in the sample frame shown above), and measures each droplet's diameter via HSV thresholding and region properties, scale-calibrated against a 100 µm reference bar, with individual droplets labeled in µm.

Wearable Microneedle for Glucose and CVD Detection

Coursework concept design
Concept design — not built Wearable biosensor architecture

What

  • A dual-biomarker wearable microneedle patch for continuous glucose and C-reactive protein (CRP) monitoring via interstitial fluid (ISF), aimed at diabetic cardiovascular disease — glucose as the metabolic marker, CRP as an inflammation marker for cardiovascular risk, read from one integrated PCB.
  • Carried from a first design through a second-generation SolidWorks CAD assembly (microneedle array, PCB, rechargeable coil, battery, support and top covers) sized for a wearable form factor.

How

  • Needles CNC-machined on PMMA for biocompatibility and mechanical stability, sputter-coated with gold for conductivity, with an Ag/AgCl reference electrode formed by chlorination; working electrode built on an AD5940 electrochemical-analog-front-end PCB.
  • Glucose sensing: electrodeposited PPD and immobilized glucose oxidase detect H₂O₂ generated by glucose oxidation, amplified and digitized on-board — the same amperometric, mediator-based detection principle (Prussian Blue-catalyzed H₂O₂ reduction) I ran and calibrated by hand in the companion lab session.
  • CRP sensing: neutravidin-anchored CRP antibodies on a separately gold-deposited site, read via electrochemical impedance spectroscopy — CRP binding changes sensor capacitance, with a target detection limit of 0.8 mg/mL in ISF-like medium, drawn from the reference literature behind the design.
  • Data transmitted wirelessly from the shared PCB to a connected device for both biomarkers.
This stayed at the system-architecture, literature-review, and CAD stage — through a second generation of the assembly — rather than a physical build. I'm including it because that translation process, from a clinical need and primary literature into electrode chemistry, PCB architecture, and a sensing circuit, is the same kind of thinking a wearable neuromodulation concept needs, even though the sensing modality is different from stimulation.

mCellSeg — Two-Stage Cell Detection & Segmentation

Independent project · public mCellSeg microscopy dataset
Built & tested Detection + segmentation

What

  • A two-stage pipeline that finds every cell in a microscopy image (YOLOv8n) and then draws its exact outline (a MedSAM model, fine-tuned for this task).

How

  • Measured on all 40 held-out validation images: detection precision 0.801, recall 0.809; 2,562 of 3,167 ground-truth cells correctly found and segmented.
  • End-to-end segmentation quality: mean Dice 0.635, median 0.766 (1.0 = perfect outline overlap), reproducibility confirmed by rerunning training with a second random seed.
  • Evaluated across both cell types in the dataset (HEK and HUVEC) and across difficulty levels, from near-perfect fields to dense, low-contrast, and confluent cases.

WBC Detection & Classification — Paper Replication

Independent project · replicating a 2025 Nature Scientific Reports paper
Built & tested Detection + classification + generalization audit

What

  • Replicated a two-stage blood-cell pipeline: YOLOv7 detects RBCs/WBCs/platelets (BCCD dataset), EfficientNetV2-S classifies each WBC into 5 subtypes (Raabin-WBC dataset) — built as a faithful replication, not a novel contribution.

How

  • Detection baseline: precision 0.823, recall 0.899, mAP@0.5 0.910. Classification baseline: 98.3% accuracy, 97.3% macro-F1 on the dataset's own held-out test split (TestA).
  • The paper's own proposed architecture improvements (attention+SIoU for detection, ASPP+balanced loss for classification) showed no detectable gain over the vanilla baselines at 3 seeds each — reported as an honest null result rather than overstated.
  • Pushed past the paper's own evaluation: on a second, different-microscope test split, both classifiers collapsed to ~14% and ~9% accuracy (from ~98%), and I traced a plausible cause to a measurable RGB color-channel shift between the two splits.
  • Also showed the classifier's confidence is decoupled from correctness under that shift (incorrect predictions averaged higher confidence than correct ones) — which is why the end-to-end pipeline's subtype predictions on BCCD (shown above) are reported as unvalidated rather than taken at face value.

In Silico CRISPR-Cas13 Guide RNA Design Pipeline

Independent project · no wet-lab component
Built & tested Bioinformatics · computational filtering

What

  • A seven-stage computational pipeline (Python, pandas, BioPython, RNAfold/ViennaRNA, Ensembl REST & Variation APIs) that generates and ranks candidate Cas13 guide RNAs against three disease-associated transcripts, to prioritize guides for synthesis before any wet-lab work.

How

  • 23-nt sliding window generated 8,264 candidate guides across the three transcripts, filtered by efficiency, off-target hits, RNA accessibility, and SNP overlap down to 334 passing all thresholds.
  • Caught two real engineering issues during QA rather than reporting the raw numbers at face value: off-target scoring currently checks each guide against one reference transcript rather than a full transcriptome (closer to a self-consistency check than a true specificity screen), and the SNP-overlap filter was silently defaulting every guide to "no overlap" instead of returning real API results — both flagged as the next hardening pass before any guide goes to synthesis.

3D Nuclei Segmentation in Human Liver Tissue

Image-analysis project · two-channel fluorescence volume
Built · result not validated Registration · correction · segmentation

What

  • An image-analysis pipeline for a 3D, two-channel (nuclei + membrane) fluorescence volume of human liver tissue, built to align the channels, remove bleed-through and uneven illumination, and segment individual nuclei so per-nucleus volume can be measured.
  • Reported as-is: the registration, crosstalk and segmentation stages run, but the illumination correction did not work, so the final 1,547 labelled objects are not validated nuclei.

How

  • Channel alignment: phase correlation on maximum-intensity projections measured the XY shift between channels (only a couple of pixels), then applied it to the whole membrane volume so the pixel-by-pixel crosstalk subtraction compares the same structures.
  • Crosstalk: nucleus-shaped dots in the membrane channel were dye bleed-through; the coefficient was estimated by least squares rather than chosen by hand (α = 1.46). An α above 1 is a warning sign, and a unit mismatch in the subtraction was traced and is queued to fix.
  • Thresholding: plain Otsu gave 0.263 because ~90% of the volume is empty background and dominates the histogram. Masking out everything below 0.18 and running Otsu on tissue pixels only moved it to 0.393, a more sensible nucleus/background split.
  • Segmentation: distance transform, marker detection and 3D watershed behaved as expected on the mask they were given.
What went wrong: the flat-field correction (divide by a Gaussian-blurred background, after 10× downsampling and sigma 30, roughly a 300 px blur on a 670 px image) produced a near-flat background, so it rescaled the image rather than correcting it and the gradient survived. The threshold therefore only cleared in the bright right-hand strip, and the nuclei in the centre of the field were missed. My "gradient removed" profile plot was also invalid evidence: wrong axis, and normalised to 0–1 against raw counts in the thousands. Fix identified: sigma ≈ 10 with no Z downsampling, then re-run; the check will be per-nucleus volumes landing near the 200–500 µm³ the literature reports for hepatocytes.

EEG Brain-Computer Interface for Motor-Imagery Rehabilitation

Co-authored · RNR 2024
Built & tested EEG · classification

What

  • EEG-based BCI classifying four motor-imagery states (left hand, right hand, jump, relax) to control a character in the game Brainrunners in real time, aimed at gamifying motor-imagery stroke rehabilitation.

How

  • 10-20 system electrodes at C3/C4 to localize left/right motor cortex; features compared across Continuous Wavelet Transform and Power Spectral Density (alpha 8–13 Hz, beta 13–30 Hz bands).
  • Random Forest (45 trees, max depth 6) outperformed SVM and k-NN; evaluated via a 200-trial, 30%-holdout train/test split with confusion matrices, precision, recall, and F1 per motor-imagery class — jump was the most reliably classified action.

GameTherapy — Gamified Stroke Rehabilitation Arm Brace

Team project (CUHK) · Medical Instrumentation & Design course, Group 1 of 7
Built & tested

What

  • An IMU + sEMG arm brace that converts elbow movement and forearm contraction into real-time WASD/Space keystrokes, so a stroke patient's rehab exercises directly control a browser game, extending Muscle Guard's EMG sensing into a full game-control input.

How

  • Gyroscope (MPU6050) tracks elbow tilt direction for WASD; a forearm sEMG channel above a set threshold triggers Space; an Arduino Leonardo with a custom shielding board and PH2.0/XH2.54 connectors reads both and emits native USB keystrokes.
  • Built and worn as a working brace, driving a racing-style browser game ("Crazy Motorcycle") as the rehab interface — demoed live at a class competition with no connection issues during trial runs or on the day.

Muscle Guard — sEMG Fatigue Detection Wearable

Independent project · Hong Kong · 2024
Built & tested Wearable electrode interface

What

  • A wearable system that monitors muscle activity via surface EMG and flags early signs of fatigue, with future NMES integration considered for recovery and relaxation.
  • 3D-printed enclosure housing an Arduino Nano, embedded into a multi-layered pocket sewn into an armband for comfort and compactness.

How

  • EMG sampled at 1000 Hz, processed in 250 ms windows to extract RMS and mean power frequency features.
  • Fatigue classified via linear regression on slope and R²; tested on 10 healthy subjects performing isometric forearm contractions.
  • 72% fatigue-detection accuracy and 262 ms processing delay — both short of target, reported as such.
